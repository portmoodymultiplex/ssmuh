The Complete Guide

SSMUH and Bill 44, explained.

The most significant change to residential zoning in a generation, and what it actually means for your lot. Written for homeowners, not planners.

The Basics

From rezoning to as-of-right

Small-scale multi-unit housing, SSMUH, is BC's framework for allowing three to six homes on residential lots that used to be zoned single-family. For decades, building a duplex or triplex on a residential lot in BC meant rezoning: a political, expensive process that could take years and still fail at a public hearing. Bill 44, passed by the province in late 2023, changed that. Municipalities were required to update their zoning bylaws by mid-2024, with some cities granted extensions into 2025, so that most residential lots now permit small-scale multi-unit housing automatically.

The practical effect: if your lot qualifies, you do not ask permission to build multiple units. You need a building permit, a design that meets your city's setback, height, and parking standards, and the financing to build. The political risk is gone. What remains is a normal construction project.

Lot Size Rules

How many units can your lot hold?

Under the provincial framework, unit minimums that municipalities must permit break down by lot size and transit proximity. Your city confirms the exact figure for your property.

Lots near SkyTrain stations may instead fall under Bill 47's Transit-Oriented Area rules, which allow significantly taller buildings under a separate framework. If you are close to a station, this is one of the first things a feasibility check confirms.

Eligibility

Does your lot qualify?

Most single-family lots do. To qualify, your property generally must be:

  • Zoned for residential use (single-family or equivalent)
  • Within the size thresholds above
  • In a municipality covered by the legislation (most BC municipalities over 5,000 people)
  • Connected or connectable to municipal water and sewer servicing
  • On a lot with legal road or lane access

Each city sets its own design standards: setbacks, height, lot coverage, parking, and tree protection all vary by municipality, and a design that works in Coquitlam may need adjusting in Port Moody. Servicing capacity, especially water and fire flow, can also shape what is buildable on a specific lot. This is exactly what a feasibility review sorts out before you spend money on drawings.

The Process

From idea to occupancy

A realistic SSMUH project runs roughly 18 to 30 months from first conversation to move-in. The phases:

01

Feasibility

Confirm zoning, unit count, servicing, and rough budget before spending anything. This is the free step.

02

Design & financing

Architect drawings that meet municipal standards, plus a financing structure that matches your goals: hold, rent, or strata and sell.

03

Permits

Building permit review. Timelines vary by city; some process SSMUH permits in a few months, others take longer.

04

Construction

Typically 10 to 16 months depending on the build form, with phased approaches possible if you are staying on site.

05

Occupancy & exit

Move family in, rent units out, or complete stratification and sell individually. The exit you planned in step one.
